How it works: each exchange, the faster and sharper fighter (speed and strategy) is more likely to attack. The attacker lands a point based on technique, speed and power against the defender’s discipline, strategy and speed. A miss can draw a counter. High-power fighters can score a 2-point knockdown, and high-resolve fighters fight harder when trailing by 3 or more. It is a toy built on the same editorial ratings as everything else here, so it shows what the ratings imply, not how the characters would really fight.

Real fights, tested against the model

Sixteen documented fights from episode recaps and fan wikis. For each, the model turns the two fighters’ average skill rating into a win probability, then we check who actually won.

How this was analyzed. I worked from published episode recaps and plot summaries, not frame-by-frame video, so outcomes and key moments are paraphrased from those. Some come from fan wikis, a few are group brawls, and one is implied by the bracket. Fight results are scripted by the writers, so this tests whether the ratings track the story, not real combat. I also set the ratings knowing how these fights ended, so this is not a blind test, and sixteen fights is still a small sample, with only three from Kickin’ It. Probability = 1 ÷ (1 + e−(rating gap ÷ 1.5)).

Sources: Looper, TV Tropes S1E10, IMDb S4E10, TV Acute S4E10, Screen Rant, Wikipedia episode list, SlashFilm, TV Tropes S4E9, The Cinemaholic, Karate Kid Wiki, Kickin’ It Wiki.
